\section{The KPS update in detail} \label{appendice:method}

\subsection{Annealed conditional chain} \label{appendice:annealed}

Discretize the reverse dynamics of \Eqref{eq:generative_SDE} over the schedule
$0 = t_0 < \dots < t_T = 1$ and write the resulting Markov chain, which runs from noise
$x_T$ down to the clean variable $x_0 = x$, as
$p(x_{0:T}) = p(x_T)\prod_{k=1}^{T} p(x_{k-1}\mid x_k)$. Conditioning the whole path on $y$
multiplies it by $p(y\mid x)/p(y)$, and that ratio telescopes along the chain,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:telescope}
    \frac{p(y \mid x_0)}{p(y)}
    = \frac{p(y \mid x_T)}{p(y)} \prod_{k=1}^{T} \frac{p(y \mid x_{k-1})}{p(y \mid x_k)} .
\end{equation}
Because the chain is Markov, $x_k$ is independent of $x_0$, hence of $y$, given $x_{k-1}$, so
each factor of the product combines with the corresponding transition into a conditional one,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:cond_transition}
    p(x_{k-1} \mid x_k, y)
    = p(x_{k-1} \mid x_k) \, \frac{p(y \mid x_{k-1})}{p(y \mid x_k)} .
\end{equation}
The leading factor of \Eqref{eq:telescope} is $p(y \mid x_T)/p(y)$, which is where
\Eqref{eq:annealed_chain} becomes an approximation: at the highest noise level $x_T$ carries
almost no information about $y$, the ratio is close to $1$, and we replace $p(x_T \mid y)$ by
the reference $p(x_T) \approx \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_1)$. No other term is approximated, and no
term couples non-adjacent levels.

The decomposition of \Eqref{eq:transition_decomposition} follows by marginalizing over the clean
variable, $p(x_{k-1} \mid x_k, y) = \int p(x \mid x_k, y)\, p(x_{k-1} \mid x, x_k, y)\,\mathrm{d}x$,
and dropping $y$ from the second factor, which is legitimate since $x_{k-1}$ is conditionally
independent of $y$ given $x$. That second factor is the diffusion bridge, available in closed
form from \Eqref{eq:transition_kernel_diffusion}, so the only object left to approximate is the
local posterior of \Eqref{eq:inner_target}.

\subsection{Prior-factor moments} \label{appendice:prior_moments}

The mean and covariance of \Eqref{eq:surrogate_prior} both come from the denoiser. Rearranging
\Eqref{eq:first_order_Tweedie} gives
$\mathbb{E}[x \mid x_t] = \alpha_t^{-1}\bigl(x_t + \sigma_t^2 \nabla_{\!x_t}\log p(x_t)\bigr)$,
and differentiating it with respect to $x_t$,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:vt_derivation}
    \nabla_{\!x_t}^{\top}\mathbb{E}[x \mid x_t]
    = \alpha_t^{-1}\bigl(I + \sigma_t^{2}\nabla^{2}_{\!x_t}\log p(x_t)\bigr) .
\end{equation}
The second-order Tweedie identity for the kernel of \Eqref{eq:transition_kernel_diffusion} is
$\mathbb{V}[x \mid x_t] = \alpha_t^{-2}\sigma_t^{2}\bigl(I + \sigma_t^{2}\nabla^{2}_{\!x_t}\log p(x_t)\bigr)$,
so combining the two eliminates the Hessian and leaves
\begin{equation} \label{eq:vt}
    V_t \;\triangleq\; \mathbb{V}[x \mid x_t] \;=\; \alpha_t^{-1}\sigma_t^{2}\,
    \nabla_{\!x_t}^{\top}\mathbb{E}[x \mid x_t] ,
\end{equation}
which is the expression used in Section~\ref{section: correction}. Only a
vector--Jacobian product through the denoiser is ever needed, never the matrix itself. Note that
$V_t$ is the covariance of the \emph{conditional} $p(x \mid x_t)$ and not of the marginal
$p(x)$: replacing it by an ensemble covariance would substitute the wrong factor into
\Eqref{eq:inner_target}.

\subsection{Gaussian surrogate} \label{appendice:surrogate}

Let $(x,y)$ given $x_t$ have moments $(\mu_x, \mu_y, C_{xx}, C_{yx}, C_{yy})$ and posit the
affine model of \Eqref{eq:surrogate_likelihood}. Matching the moments it implies against those
of the joint,
\begin{equation} \label{eq:moment_match}
    \mathbb{E}[y] = A\mu_x + b, \qquad
    \mathrm{Cov}(y,x) = A\,C_{xx}, \qquad
    \mathrm{Cov}(y) = A\,C_{xx}A^{\top} + \Sigma_y ,
\end{equation}
and solving in order yields \Eqref{eq:slr}. The pseudo-inverse in $A = C_{yx}C_{xx}^{\dagger}$
is needed because $C_{xx}$ is estimated from $N$ particles and has rank at most $N-1 \ll n$;
$A$ is then the linear minimum-mean-square predictor of $y$ from $x$ on the subspace the
ensemble spans, and is left at zero off it.

Two properties matter downstream. First, nothing assumes the noise $\eta$ of
\Eqref{eq:forward_model} is additive or Gaussian: the surrogate matches moments, whatever their
origin, and $\Sigma_y$ absorbs both the stochasticity of $\mathcal{A}$ and the residual of the
affine fit, which is why it cannot be replaced by a nominal noise covariance. Second, the
identity $\Sigma_y = C_{yy} - AC_{xx}A^{\top}$ holds only at the least-squares $A$; neither
variant uses that $A$, which is why $\Sigma_y$ is estimated separately in
Appendix~\ref{appendice:sigma_estimation} rather than read off \Eqref{eq:slr}.

\subsection{Ensemble realization} \label{appendice:ensemble}

\paragraph{Drawing the ensemble.} \label{appendice:draw}
Each particle is denoised from its own anchor by integrating \Eqref{eq:generative_SDE} at
$\lambda = 0$ from $t$ down to $0$, costing a few denoiser evaluations and no simulator calls.
The draw must be a \emph{sample} of $p(x \mid x_t)$ rather than the Tweedie mean: the surrogate
of \Eqref{eq:slr} is estimated from the scatter of the pairs $\{x^{(i)}, y^{(i)}\}$, and a cloud
collapsed onto the conditional mean carries no scatter to fit. We use the deterministic
probability-flow limit rather than a stochastic reversal; both are exact reversals of
\Eqref{eq:forward_diffusion_process}, but the stochastic one re-injects noise that the denoiser
must then remove with its own approximate score.

\paragraph{Moment matching.} \label{appendice:moment_matching}
\Eqref{eq:ensemble_update} reproduces the moments of \Eqref{eq:kalman} without forming either.
With $x^{(i)} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu_t, V_t)$ and
$y^{(i)} = Ax^{(i)} + b + \varepsilon^{(i)}$, $\varepsilon^{(i)} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \Sigma_y)$,
\begin{align} \label{eq:update_moments}
    \mathbb{E}\bigl[x^{(i)+}\bigr] &= \mu_t + K(y - A\mu_t - b), \\
    \mathrm{Cov}\bigl(x^{(i)+}\bigr)
      &= (I - KA)V_t(I - KA)^{\top} + K\Sigma_y K^{\top} = (I - KA)V_t ,
\end{align}
the last equality holding for the gain of \Eqref{eq:kalman}. The cross term vanishes because the
simulator is queried \emph{with} its noise: the innovation $y - y^{(i)}$ already carries a draw
of $\mathcal{N}(0,\Sigma_y)$ independent of $x^{(i)}$. This is what removes the perturbation and
the inflation a stochastic ensemble Kalman filter would otherwise need.

\paragraph{Incremental versus anchored updates.} \label{appendice:anchoring}
Each particle moves from its own position. The anchored alternative
$x^{(i)+} = \mu_t + K(y - y^{(i)})$ places every particle at a common point plus a
residual-driven offset, so its analysis covariance is $K\Sigma_yK^{\top}$ alone: the prior
spread $V_t$ is discarded rather than contracted, and the ensemble collapses. The distinction is
a property of the update \emph{form}, not of the quality of its ingredients --- the anchored form
collapses even with an exact $A$ and an exact $\Sigma_y$, whereas \Eqref{eq:ensemble_update}
retains $(I - KA)V_t$, the posterior covariance of the surrogate.

\subsection{Low-rank estimation and linear solves} \label{appendice:lowrank}

\paragraph{KPS-Hybrid.} The fitted slope is kept factored, $A = W V_h^{\top}$ with $r$ columns,
never as a $d \times n$ matrix. With $M = V_h^{\top} V_t V_h$, $G = W^{\top}W$ and
$\Sigma_y = \sigma_y^{2}I$, the push-through identity
\begin{equation} \label{eq:pushthrough}
    W^{\top}\bigl(W M W^{\top} + \sigma_y^{2} I\bigr)^{-1}
    = \bigl(\sigma_y^{2} I_r + G M\bigr)^{-1} W^{\top}
\end{equation}
reduces the whole step to one $r \times r$ solve. The equivalent Woodbury form is algebraically
identical but numerically fragile here: it recovers the step as a difference divided by
$\sigma_y^{2}$, and since $AV_tA^{\top}$ has rank $r$ while $d$ is in the thousands,
$\sigma_y^{2}I$ is the only term making the system invertible and that difference is a near-exact
cancellation over the remaining $d - r$ directions, so dividing it by a small $\sigma_y^{2}$
amplifies the rounding error by $1/\sigma_y^{2}$. \Eqref{eq:pushthrough} never divides by
$\sigma_y^{2}$. We additionally project $M$ onto the positive semi-definite cone, which is
required rather than cosmetic: $V_t$ of \Eqref{eq:vt} is the Jacobian of a learned network and is
not symmetric, so $M$ can be indefinite, and an exact solve of an indefinite system returns an
amplifying step rather than a gain.

\paragraph{KPS-Gradient.} Here $A$ is implicit and admits no low-rank factorization, so the
inverse is applied by GMRES \citep{GMRES} on $u \mapsto A V_t A^{\top} u + \sigma_y^{2} u$, each
iteration costing one Jacobian-vector and one vector-Jacobian product through the simulator and
one through the denoiser. GMRES rather than conjugate gradients because the operator is not
symmetric: for an exact denoiser $V_t$ would be proportional to a Hessian by \Eqref{eq:vt}, but a
learned network's Jacobian is the Hessian of nothing. CG assumes symmetry to keep its search
directions conjugate and is unstable here, whereas GMRES assumes neither symmetry nor
definiteness.

\subsection{Estimating \texorpdfstring{$\Sigma_y$}{Sigma y} out of sample}
\label{appendice:sigma_estimation}

$\Sigma_y$ is the residual covariance of the fit and cannot be read off \Eqref{eq:slr}, which is
exact only at the least-squares $A$ --- KPS-Hybrid truncates to rank $r$ and KPS-Gradient uses a
Jacobian, so neither qualifies. Nor can it be estimated in sample: the regression is solved at
the full numerical rank of the centred ensemble, which is $N - 1$ whenever $N - 1 \le n$, so the
fit interpolates its own pairs and the in-sample residual is zero to machine precision however
poor the linearization actually is.

We therefore split the ensemble in two, fit on one half, score the squared residual on the other,
swap the folds and average, taking $\Sigma_y = \sigma_y^{2}I$. This costs no additional simulator
calls, both folds reusing the same $\{x^{(i)}, y^{(i)}\}$. The resulting $\sigma_y^{2}$ is the
only channel through which the surrogate learns how badly the affine model fits, and it is what
keeps the gain from over-stepping at the low-noise end of the schedule, where that fit is worst
relative to the remaining prior spread.

\subsection{Gibbs loop at fixed noise level} \label{appendice:gibbs}

\paragraph{Invariance.} \label{appendice:gibbs_invariance}
\Eqref{eq:gibbs_sweep} is a two-block Gibbs sampler on $p(x, x_t \mid y)$: the first block
redraws $x$ from $p(x \mid x_t, y)$, the second redraws $x_t$ from
$p(x_t \mid x, y) = p(x_t \mid x)$, the equality holding because $x_t$ is conditionally
independent of $y$ given $x$ and the kernel being that of
\Eqref{eq:transition_kernel_diffusion}. Each block leaves the joint invariant, hence so does the
sweep, and in particular the marginal $p(x_t \mid y)$ that \Eqref{eq:annealed_chain} assumes at
every level is preserved. With the surrogate of \Eqref{eq:kalman} in place of the exact first
block the sweep is invariant for the Gaussian surrogate rather than for the true conditional, so
the loop should be read as a mechanism for restoring consistency and not as a route to asymptotic
exactness.

\paragraph{Accumulation of per-level errors.} \label{appendice:gibbs_perturbation}
The loop does not reduce the bias of the update at a given level. What it removes is the drift of
the \emph{pair}: after a correction acting on $x$ alone, $(x, x_t)$ is no longer a plausible
realization of \Eqref{eq:forward_diffusion_process}, while \Eqref{eq:annealed_chain} assumes at
every level that it is. Re-noising from the corrected $x$ returns the pair to the forward
process, so the mismatch is not carried into the next transition and errors do not compound over
the $T$ levels. A second effect is that the surrogate is refitted around a cloud that already
carries $y$, so the linearization is taken where the posterior has mass rather than where the
prior does, and the re-noising restores the spread the update contracted, which is what lets the
chain reach modes the unimodal \Eqref{eq:kalman} cannot.

\paragraph{Advancing the chain.} \label{appendice:transfer}
On the last sweep of a level the cloud is re-noised at the \emph{next} level's noise scale rather
than the current one (Algorithm~\ref{alg:KPS}, lines 5--9). This is how information survives a
level transition: re-noising at the current scale and then re-drawing at the next would discard
the correction just applied, whereas the handover delivers the cloud to the next level already at
the tighter scale, carrying the update with it.

\subsection{Variants and cost} \label{appendice:variants}

The variants differ only in how the slope $A$ is obtained and agree in every other component ---
prior draw, $V_t$, held-out $\Sigma_y$, incremental update and Gibbs loop.

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
\toprule
 & KPS-Hybrid & KPS-Gradient \\
\midrule
slope $A$       & regression on $\{x^{(i)}, y^{(i)}\}$ & Jacobian of $\mathcal{A}$ \\
simulator       & black-box, non-differentiable        & must be differentiable \\
rank of $A$     & at most $\min(r, N-1)$               & full \\
linear solve    & $r \times r$, \Eqref{eq:pushthrough} & GMRES, matrix-free \\
per-iteration   & ---                                  & one jvp and one vjp \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

One call to \textsc{Update} costs $N$ prior draws and $N$ simulations, and the chain makes
$M \times T$ such calls, so the simulator budget is exactly $M\,T\,N$ and does not depend on the
variant. Prior draws cost a few denoiser evaluations each and consume no simulator budget.
KPS-Gradient adds two simulator differentiations and one denoiser differentiation per Krylov
iteration; these do not enter the simulator-call budget, but they dominate its wall time, and the
number of iterations is the single setting that most affects its accuracy.

The per-particle linearization $A^{(i)}$ of Section~\ref{section: correction} costs one
Jacobian-vector product per particle instead of the $N$ that assembling the ensemble average of
\Eqref{eq:stein} would require, and is obtained for free by batching the differentiation over the
cloud. The particles then couple only through $\sigma_y^{2}$.
